About VForensiQ

The browser front end for Paper 1 — Design and Development of a Video Forgery Model Using Deep Learning with Attention Mechanisms (SMA-CLMPNet).

🎯 What it runs

The classical half of the published pipeline, faithful to the research code: gradient-based key-frame selection (GetPreprocessing.py), Viola-Jones face ROI, Lucas-Kanade optical flow, LDZP texture (LDZP.py), 3×3 neighbourhood statistics and GLCM descriptors (GetFeatures.py).

⚠️ What it does not claim

There is no trained SMA-CLMPNet checkpoint in the repository — Analysis.py trains every model on the fly and saves only metrics. This app therefore reports no "authentic vs forged" verdict from the published network. The index it shows is a transparent heuristic over the signals it actually measures.

📐 How the index is computed

Three measured terms, each squashed to 0–1 and combined with fixed weights that are returned with every response so the score can be audited: key-frame discontinuity (0.40), optical-flow irregularity (0.35) and residual-energy dispersion (0.25). Peaks above z = 2.5 are flagged on the chart.

🧠 Deep stages

Grad-CAM, ResNet-101 statistical features and VGG-16 + LDZP need 733 MB of Keras weights — ResNet-101 alone is 180 MB and VGG-16 553 MB, both instantiated at module scope in GetFeatures.py. They exceed a 512 MB instance, so they are gated behind ENABLE_DEEP=1 and reported as unavailable otherwise.

⚙️ Runtime

Flask + OpenCV + NumPy + SciPy only — deliberately no TensorFlow at request time, so the container stays small and cold starts stay fast. Frames are downscaled to 480 px wide and capped at MAX_FRAMES (default 48) to bound memory and latency.

🔒 Privacy

Uploads are written to a temporary path, analysed in-process, and deleted in the request's finally block. Nothing is persisted; every result image is returned inline as a base64 data URI rather than stored on disk.
